# Design

Switchboard's visual world since world v2 (2026-11-21): **a sun-soaked coastal city at golden hour sliding into neon night**, a homage to the open-world crime-game genre that never names the game (see PRODUCT.md, Brand Commitments). The 3D city is the dashboard; a quiet HUD floats over it. Cinematic stylised realism: realistic proportions, PBR materials, sky lighting, soft shadows, haze, bloom and a teal-orange grade. Not toy-like, not flat low-poly, not photoreal.

The working agreement behind this file, with the contracts between the parts, is `docs/world-v2/BRIEF.md`.

## The city (client/src/world)

- **Geography:** the ocean lies toward −Z, the city runs inland toward +Z, and a bay with a pastel skyline of towers sits toward +X. A promenade with a low stucco wall and pavers separates the beach road from the sand.
- **The district:** 16 plots of 20 × 16 m on a 4 × 4 street grid, each with its own Miami Deco or MiMo building (hotel, beach café, diner and more), generated by `tools/assets/deco`. Each git repository takes the next free plot and keeps it; a repo with no sessions keeps it 7 days with its tag dimmed. Sessions outside any repo share the **beach bar** on the sand; repos beyond 16 share the **co-working** building.
- **A lived-in plot:** windows and neon come on, terrace string lights glow after dark with soft light pools under them, laptop screens light up, and a neon outline traces the plot in the repo's colour. A vacant plot sits shuttered and dark.
- **Plot tag:** the repo name and an icon for its kind (web = globe, mobile = phone, docs = book, data = pipeline, other = branch) on dark glass with a hairline and glow in the repo's neon colour. "+N" counts agents beyond the plot's six spots. Tags that collide with a nearer tag or a badge dim so the nearest and the urgent stay readable.
- **Around it:** background blocks, the skyline, palms that sway, rippling water with shoreline foam, and background life (traffic, boats, people walking, a helicopter, a blimp). Only agents stand on a glowing plot, carry a tag and can be clicked.

## People

One person per agent, from a cast of 20 motion-captured characters (Rocketbox, MIT), chosen from the session id and unique among active agents. Their name label is the session title, shown when close, hovered or selected; there are no made-up names.

Pink is reserved for "needs you"; repo colours come from a separate neon set without it.

## Times of day

Day, Sunset (default), Night and Auto (follows the local clock), blended over about two seconds. Each look sets the sky dome, sun, hemisphere and image-based light, fog, exposure, the grade, window and neon strength, terrace lights and wet-look asphalt. The states read the same in all of them.

## Camera

A damped three-quarter camera, tilt 25°–65° (starts at 40°), drag to pan, right-drag or two fingers to turn, wheel or pinch to zoom toward the pointer. Clicking someone flies a crane-like arc to a 27° close-up and follows them until you pan; the framing shifts so they sit in the space the panels leave free. A height map of the buildings keeps the camera out of them: it moves in front of a building rather than jumping over it. After 2 minutes idle, slow drifting shots cut (through a short dip to black) to whoever needs you, every other shot; any input hands the camera back.

## HUD (client/src/styles)

- **Surfaces:** dark plum glass (`rgb(20 12 34 / 0.9)`) with a hairline, a soft offset shadow and a blur behind, used only where text must stay legible over the city.
- **Type:** the wordmark is a warm-white neon script (Yellowtail, Apache 2.0, self-hosted) with a coral glow, like a motel sign. Anton (OFL, self-hosted) is the display voice: panel titles, section titles, counts and the clock, in capitals. The system sans is for reading; monospace only for commands, branches and models.
- **Accent:** warm white `#fff1e2` for the primary button and the active rail item; state colours only where they mean a state (dots, badges, the state line).
- **Layout:** wordmark top left (with a Demo or connection note), a rail of views on the left (Map, Projects, Agents, Inbox with a count, Archive, Settings), counts, time of day with the clock, sound and help top right, the round minimap bottom left, a controls hint bottom right that fades once each control has been used, toasts top centre.
- **Agent card:** anchored beside the selected person (flips sides near an edge, docks right when they're off screen): title, repo and branch, a state line with the time that matters (waiting, working, idle), then what they need with its buttons (Allow once / Always allow / Deny, the question's options and Send answer, Approve / Approve with auto-accept / Keep planning, Mark read, Reply), and Open in cmux and Expand.
- **Side panel:** the request in full (whole plan or reply), the recent conversation as bubbles, the reply box (only while the agent is idle; ⌘ Enter sends), details (model, mode, context, folder, branch, ports, turn time, a 3-hour bar chart of tool calls), Open in cmux, Mark read, Archive.
- **Never missed:** the beam, edge arrows with the badge for off-screen requests (they merge when close and keep off the minimap), minimap blips pinned to its rim, the Inbox count, the tab-title count and an optional chime.
- **Under 860 px** (container queries on the app, so a narrow cmux split counts): the rail shows icons only, the clock hides, the side panel takes the full height; under 700 px the time-of-day control moves to Settings.

## Sound

Muted by default and synthesised in the page (no audio files): a low wash of waves with a breath of city, and a soft two-note bell when someone starts needing you.

## Motion

One authored moment: the city reveal, when the title screen fades and the HUD settles in. Elsewhere motion explains: the camera flight to a person, the card scaling in from a blur, the side panel sliding in, toasts dropping in. People animate with motion capture and eased blends. Reduced motion (system setting, or forced on or off in Settings) holds the camera cuts instant, stops bobbing, swaying and drifting, and turns off the idle camera. The renderer drops to about 30 fps once the camera settles and the pointer rests (10 with reduced motion).
